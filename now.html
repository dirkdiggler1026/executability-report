WHAT COMES BACK WHEN YOU SELL

Not the quoted price. The result of a full round trip, inside one pool.

Buy a tokenised stock with USDG and sell the whole position straight back through the same pool, at four sizes, at one pinned block every 30 minutes. This page is the dated series, regenerated daily; the report page is a different window. That page is a frozen snapshot of 12 rounds ending at block 54,270,401 (2026-09-04) and does not change after publication. A figure that differs between the two pages is a difference of window, not of method.
Known limitation, stated here rather than in a footnote elsewhere. Every row is the best route among the pools in the published pool table, and that table keeps the eight best pools per token. A pool created after it is invisible here.
* QQQ at $100. This cell is one of the ten errors in the published archive. The pool table ends at block 53,983,886; a cheaper 0.05% QQQ/USDG pool was created at block 55,328,824, after that cutoff, so the series routes around it. Measured against the chain, this cell is about 95.6%. The damage is bounded and the bound is checkable: that pool returns no quote above about $300, so the $1,000 row and every deeper size are unaffected by it.
What is not established: whether other rows are affected by other post-cutoff pools. That is the post-cutoff pool scan, and the repository says so rather than implying this is the only one.
The failure is not one thing. Some tokens collapse at every size with almost no variance; some switch between two states with nothing in between; some slope down as size rises. The range above shows how far each cell moved within the day — it is not a characterisation of the token. A token that alternates between two states can look constant on any single day, and one that is quiet today can be wide tomorrow. Which shape a token has is a property of the series, not of one day; it is established in the report, not here.
