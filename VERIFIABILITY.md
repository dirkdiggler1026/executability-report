# Which venues can be verified, and which can only be trusted

_Generated by `code/make_verifiability.py` from [coverage.json](coverage.json). Every verdict
below is a field in that file; none is typed here._

The quantity this project publishes — what a position actually realises on exit — can only be
published where it can be **read from public contract state by anyone, with no account**. That
is not a preference. A figure obtained through an account cannot be rechecked by the reader, so
it cannot carry a priced deliverable, and a figure that is a market maker's momentary quote is
not a reserve that anyone can read at all.

That condition is becoming a property of the permissionless venues rather than an industry
norm. A regulated venue's disclosure is auditable by its regulator, which is not the same thing
as verifiable by a reader. So the useful thing to keep is not an opinion about which venues are
better, but a dated record of which ones can be checked and which ones cannot — and for the
second kind, the structural reason.

### Coinbase-issued equity wrappers on Base — yes

Uniswap v3-shaped pools; reserves, tick bitmap and per-tick liquidity are readable at a pinned block hash with requireCanonical, by anyone, with no account.

Published measurements: `measurements/base-depth`, `measurements/base-depth-recheck-2026-10-09`

### Pendle PT market for ORBIO on Robinhood Chain — yes

the market's whole state is one readState call and the pricing curve is closed form, so a quote is arithmetic rather than a walk; reads pin by block hash with requireCanonical, which this endpoint honours - a forged hash returns 'header not found'. The constraint is retention, not permission: state survives only minutes on the public endpoint, so a pre-expiry capture cannot be made after the fact and is archived at the time instead.

Published measurements: `measurements/orbio-pendle`

### Robinhood Chain pools — yes

pool state is readable by anyone from the public endpoint; the constraint here is retention rather than permission - the public endpoint keeps only minutes of state, so historical rounds need an archive and that is disclosed with the series.

Published measurements: `data`, `measurements/oneside-depth`

### Backed / xStocks — unknown

no enumerable on-chain registry was found for this issuer's token set; addresses are reachable only by cross-chain identity, so the venue set cannot be enumerated reproducibly and no depth figure is published.

No depth figure is published for this venue.

### Securitize Stocks — no

announced 2026-10-08 with three properties that each remove the readable quantity: access is gated behind KYC and a qualified-investor test, so a quote cannot be obtained without an account; trading runs on the issuer's own proprietary AMM rather than a pool whose reserves are public; and liquidity is provided by a designated market maker, which means the depth is a quote that exists off-chain for milliseconds rather than a reserve that can be read. The honest deliverable for a venue like this is not a number, it is the finding that its depth is not independently verifiable, with the reason stated.

No depth figure is published for this venue.

The two halves of this file are the point. Depth that can be read from public state is becoming a property of the permissionless venues rather than an industry norm: a regulated venue's disclosure is auditable by its regulator, which is not the same as verifiable by a reader. Nothing here says the permissioned venues are worse or thinner - I cannot measure them, and that is the finding.

## What this list is not

It is not a ranking, and a verdict of `no` is not a claim that a venue is thin. For the venues
marked `no` I have no depth measurement and cannot obtain one, which is the entire finding:
anyone sizing a limit against such a venue is choosing to trust a figure rather than check one,
and that choice should at least be visible.

Rows marked `announcement` are taken from public announcements and have **not** been
independently verified here. They record that a venue exists and why its depth is not
checkable. They say nothing about its actual depth.

## The rule this file enforces

A venue that names measurement paths must be readable from public state. A venue that is not
must name none, and every directory under `measurements/` is either claimed by a venue row or
listed as not being a venue-depth measurement — so nothing sits in this tree unclassified.
Check K in `code/check_published.py` fails if any of that stops being true.
